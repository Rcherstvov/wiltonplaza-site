The Short Answer: A Street Address Is Not Automatic Approval

Banks must identify and verify customers. The federal Customer Identification Program rule requires a bank's written procedures to collect identifying information and verify identity using risk-based methods. For a customer that is not an individual, the address entry may concern a principal place of business, local office, or other physical location.

That federal baseline does not create a universal “virtual address accepted” rule. Published policies illustrate the difference: Chase lists a business address among application information, while Wells Fargo says virtual addresses and P.O./commercial mailboxes do not satisfy its required physical address. Policies can differ by institution, account, industry, and application channel.

What Does “Virtual Business Address” Mean to a Bank?

“Virtual business address” is a service-industry label, not a single banking category. Services and buildings differ, and a bank decides how the particular arrangement fits its policy.

A street-format address can be more useful than a P.O. Box for websites, invoices, and permitted public filings, but its appearance does not prove that the company physically operates there. Describe the arrangement accurately. At Wilton Plaza, the Business Address plan provides authorized use under an agreement and receive-and-hold service. It is not a private office lease.

What Documents Should You Prepare?

Account-opening lists vary. Confirm the current list directly with the institution and ask whether it requires copies, originals, certified records, recently dated documents, or a branch visit.

  • Government-issued identification. Bring the identification requested for every owner, controlling person, and signer who must participate.
  • Tax identification. Depending on the entity and account, this may be an EIN assignment notice or an owner's Social Security number.
  • Formation documents. A Florida LLC may need filed Articles of Organization or another Sunbiz record showing legal existence.
  • Ownership or authority records. An operating agreement, resolutions, or partnership records may help show who can open and control the account.
  • Licenses and fictitious-name records. Regulated businesses and companies using a DBA may need the corresponding filings.
  • Address evidence. The bank may request a utility bill, lease, state filing, insurance record, or another document from its approved list.
  • Signed address agreement. Banks commonly ask for the signed agreement to confirm that the company is authorized to use a business address and to understand the service. Keep the complete, current agreement available.

A signed address agreement is supporting evidence, not a bank approval letter. The bank may still require proof of the company's physical operating location or decline to accept the agreement for verification.

Why Address Consistency Helps—but Accuracy Comes First

Bank reviewers may compare the application with Sunbiz records, the EIN notice, licenses, the company's website, and ownership documents. Use the same legal name and formatting where the underlying facts are the same.

Do not force every address to match. Sunbiz principal-office, mailing, and registered-agent fields serve different purposes, as explained in Florida LLC principal address vs. registered-agent address. The IRS also distinguishes mailing and physical-location information. Give the bank the specific address it requests and disclose a separate operating or residential address when required.

A Practical Checklist Before Applying

  1. Choose the bank and account first. Review the institution's current eligibility, address, documentation, opening-deposit, and application-channel rules.
  2. Ask an exact question. Describe the authorized commercial business-address service and ask which fields it may fill.
  3. Activate before use. Finish onboarding and receive written authorization before putting the address on Sunbiz, an EIN record, a website, or a bank application.
  4. Reconcile the records. Check the legal name, EIN, DBA, owners, and addresses across the documents the bank will see.
  5. Bring the signed agreement. Keep the full address agreement and activation record with the formation and tax documents.
  6. Explain the operating model truthfully. Be ready to state where work is performed, where records are kept, and where customers are served.
  7. Provide personal details separately. A business address does not replace a residential address or identity document requested for an individual.
  8. Plan to collect mail. Wilton Plaza receives and holds mail for in-person pickup. Retrieve bank correspondence promptly; no provider assumes your response deadlines.
  9. Compare when needed. If one bank's policy does not fit, consider another institution rather than mischaracterizing the address.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a virtual business address to open a bank account in Florida?

Possibly. A bank may accept an authorized commercial business address for one or more address fields, but each institution applies its own account-opening and verification rules. Ask the bank what it accepts before applying and provide every physical-location or owner address it separately requests.

What proof of a business address might a bank request?

Requirements vary, but a bank may ask for formation records, an EIN notice, a business license, recent statements, or a signed address agreement showing the company is authorized to use the address. The agreement supports the application; it does not require a bank to approve the address or account.

Will a business address keep my home address from the bank?

Not necessarily. The bank may separately require the residential address and identification of owners, controlling persons, or authorized signers. A public-facing business address does not replace identity-verification information requested privately by the bank.

Should my bank, Sunbiz, and EIN addresses all match?

Consistency can reduce questions when the same address truthfully answers each field, but the forms do not always ask for the same thing. Use the correct legal name, disclose the business’s actual facts, and explain legitimate differences rather than forcing an inaccurate match.
