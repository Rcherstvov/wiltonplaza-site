A virtual-office label does not change those facts or guarantee an IRS result.

Is an EIN Florida-Specific?

No. An EIN is the federal tax identification number the IRS assigns to businesses and other entities. Florida owners follow the same federal application framework as applicants elsewhere in the United States. Forming an LLC through Sunbiz and applying for an EIN are separate steps handled by separate agencies.

An EIN does not prove that a business occupies a location, establish a Florida license, or convert an address service into a physical office. The application establishes a federal tax account from information supplied by the applicant. The useful question is not simply, “Can I use a virtual address for an EIN?” It is, “What address does the IRS request in this field, and what are my company's actual facts?”

Which Address Goes on Form SS-4?

The current IRS Instructions for Form SS-4 distinguish three address-related entries. Understanding them prevents a common mistake: treating “mailing address,” “street address,” and “primary physical location” as three names for the same thing.

SS-4 field What the IRS asks for When a business address may fit
Lines 4a–4b Mailing address for the entity's correspondence When use is authorized and held mail can be collected reliably
Lines 5a–5b Physical street address, only if different from the mailing address Only when it truthfully is the entity's physical address; no P.O. Box
Line 6 County and state of the primary physical location Only when the company's actual primary location supports that entry

Lines 4a–4b: Mailing Address

These lines ask for the mailing address for the entity's correspondence. The instructions say this address generally will be used on the entity's tax returns. Choose it deliberately. It should be an address the business is authorized to use and where important correspondence can be retrieved reliably.

If a provider's agreement permits IRS correspondence, understand exactly what happens after delivery. At Wilton Plaza, business mail is received during business hours and held for pickup under the agreement. Confirm the specific intended use in writing before listing any provider's address on a tax form. The owner remains responsible for retrieving held mail and meeting every deadline.

Lines 5a–5b: Street Address

The IRS asks for the entity's physical address on these lines only when it differs from the mailing address. The instructions say not to enter a P.O. Box in the street-address field. A commercial street address and a P.O. Box are therefore not interchangeable.

A street-format address also should not be entered merely because it looks more professional. The applicant remains responsible for giving a truthful physical address based on the entity's situation. Confirm with a tax professional if a remote, home-based, mobile, or multi-location business is unsure which physical address applies.

Line 6: County and State of the Principal Business

Line 6 asks for the county and state where the principal business is located, which the IRS instructions describe as the entity's primary physical location. A Broward County mailing address does not by itself establish that Broward is the primary physical location. Use the facts, not a desired marketing geography.

These federal entries are different from the state filing fields. See Florida LLC principal address vs. registered-agent address before assuming your Sunbiz and IRS entries should match.

Can I Use a Virtual Address for an EIN?

“Virtual address” and “virtual office” are service-industry terms, not Form SS-4 categories. The IRS instructions focus on the mailing address, physical street address, and primary physical location. A plan name does not override those distinctions.

Start with the core service: a professional business address authorized under a written agreement. A virtual office is a secondary option that may add meeting-room or workplace features. For EIN purposes, extra amenities do not change the accuracy standard. What matters is whether the particular address use is truthful and permitted.

When a Business Address May Be a Practical Fit

A professional address can be useful for a Broward business that does not need a dedicated office but wants a stable, consistent public presence. Common examples include consultants who work at client sites, remote professional-service firms, independent contractors, and owners who want to reduce unnecessary use of a residential address on public-facing materials.

It may also simplify recordkeeping when the same authorized address appears on the company's website, proposals, invoices, and other ordinary business materials. Consistency is useful, but it does not mean every government form must contain the same entry. Different forms ask different questions. Accuracy comes first.

A business-address agreement is not a lease, dedicated workspace, or evidence that staff regularly work at the location. Do not describe the arrangement as something it is not. If the company needs operational premises, public access, records storage, signage, or an industry-specific office, review those requirements separately.

What a Business Address Does Not Guarantee

  • No automatic EIN result. The IRS decides whether an application is complete and whether more information is needed.
  • No substitute for the responsible party. Form SS-4 separately asks for the individual who ultimately owns, controls, or effectively directs the entity, subject to the form's rules.
  • No change to the company's real location. Purchasing an address does not make Wilton Manors the primary physical location if the facts say otherwise.
  • No universal third-party approval. An EIN assignment and later third-party reviews are separate processes with their own requirements.
  • No registered-agent appointment. A business-address plan does not authorize service of process or create a Florida registered agent.

A Step-by-Step Checklist for Florida Owners

  1. Use the current IRS application. Start at IRS.gov and review the current online process or Form SS-4 instructions rather than relying on an old checklist.
  2. Match the legal name. Enter the entity's legal name exactly as it appears on the applicable formation document. Put a different trade name only where the application requests it.
  3. Map the real locations. Write down the correspondence address, physical street address, and primary physical county and state before completing the application.
  4. Activate the address first. Do not enter a provider's location before onboarding is complete and you have written authorization to use it.
  5. Confirm the intended use. Read the agreement and ask whether the precise IRS use you are considering is permitted. Do not infer permission from a marketing label.
  6. Plan for correspondence. If mail will be received and held, decide who will pick it up and how often. Tax deadlines remain the company's responsibility.
  7. Review before submitting. Check the legal name, entity type, responsible party, reason for applying, addresses, and principal activity as one consistent record.
  8. Save the documents. Retain the submitted information, EIN assignment notice, activation notice, and the signed address agreement banks commonly ask for.
  9. Report later changes. The IRS directs businesses to Form 8822-B for later changes to an address, location, or responsible party. Follow the current instructions for the change being reported.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a business address be the mailing address on an EIN application?

It may be when the business is authorized to use it for IRS correspondence and can retrieve held mail reliably. Confirm the provider’s written terms first and follow the current IRS instructions.

Can I put a P.O. Box on Form SS-4?

The IRS form allows a P.O. Box in the mailing-address entry. The instructions say not to use a P.O. Box for the separate physical street-address field.

Can I use a virtual address for an EIN?

A virtual address may fit the mailing-address field when its use is authorized, but “virtual address” is a service label, not an IRS category or approval guarantee. The physical-address and primary-location entries must reflect the business’s actual facts.

Should my EIN address match my Sunbiz address?

Use the same legal name and maintain accurate records, but answer each agency’s actual question. Sunbiz and the IRS use distinct forms and address fields. Do not force a match when the truthful answers differ.
