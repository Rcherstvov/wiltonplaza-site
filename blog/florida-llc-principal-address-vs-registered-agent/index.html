Florida LLC Principal Address vs. Registered Agent Address: What’s the Difference?

Published July 22, 2026 · By Wilton Plaza

Short answer: a Florida LLC's principal address identifies its principal office. Its registered-agent address is the Florida street address where the appointed agent accepts service of process. Its mailing address is for correspondence. The same location can fill more than one role only when each use independently qualifies.

This guide focuses narrowly on those three Sunbiz fields for owners in Broward County and across Florida. For the full sequence, see how to form an LLC in Florida. For the separate legal requirement, read whether your Florida LLC needs a registered agent.

The Short Answer: Principal Address vs. Registered-Agent Address

The Florida Division of Corporations' LLC filing instructions treat the fields separately. Copying one address into every field can create a public record that does not match how the company operates.

What Is a Principal Address for a Florida LLC?

Sunbiz defines the principal place of business address as the street address of the LLC's principal office. In practical terms, it is the location the company identifies as its main business address on the state record. The instructions call for a street address rather than a P.O. Box.

For a home-based consultant in Fort Lauderdale, that could be the owner's residence. For an owner who wants a separate commercial presence, it could be a professional business address in Wilton Manors, provided the agreement permits the intended use and the entry accurately represents the LLC's circumstances. A virtual office is a secondary service term you may see for plans that add amenities to the business address.

The principal address is part of the public-facing company record. Before entering any location, ask:

  • Is this a street address that the LLC is authorized to use?
  • Does the written agreement cover use on the intended business filing?
  • Will the location remain stable through the company's next annual report?
  • Does a license, permit, lease, or professional rule impose a more specific requirement?

A business address supports a professional public presence; it does not create operational facts. Businesses subject to on-site, records, signage, or industry rules should review them before choosing an address.

What Is a Registered-Agent Address in Florida?

A registered agent is the individual or legal entity appointed to accept service of process for the LLC. The registered-agent address is the registered office where that function is performed. Unlike the principal-office instructions, the Sunbiz registered-agent section expressly requires a physical street address in Florida and says not to list a P.O. Box.

The agent must also accept the appointment. On the Articles of Organization, the registered agent's signature confirms familiarity with and acceptance of the statutory obligations. Florida permits an individual associated with the business to serve, and an active Florida business entity may also serve. The LLC being formed cannot name itself as its own registered agent.

This is more than an address rental. The agent must be prepared to receive service of process at the registered office. A standard business-address agreement should never be assumed to include that legal role. If a provider offers registered-agent service, it should be a separately confirmed service with its own acceptance and terms.

Can a Florida LLC Use a P.O. Box?

Florida also asks for the LLC's mailing address if it differs from the principal address. Sunbiz expressly notes that a P.O. Box is acceptable in the mailing-address field. That does not make a P.O. Box the principal office or the registered office.

Owners remain responsible for collecting held items and meeting every response or filing deadline. The service is limited to that receive-and-hold workflow.

Can the Principal Address and Registered-Agent Address Be the Same?

They can point to the same street location when the facts support both entries, but they remain separate designations. For example, an owner might maintain the LLC's principal office at a Florida commercial address while a qualified registered agent independently operates at that location and accepts the appointment. The shared location does not merge the two roles.

The reverse is also common: a Broward business may use a professional address for its principal office and appoint a registered-agent company at another Florida address. Sunbiz expressly requires the registered office to be in Florida; its principal-office instruction requires a street address but states no Florida-location restriction. Do not copy one field into another without checking the company's facts.

Privacy: What a Professional Address Can and Cannot Do

Using a professional business address can keep a residence out of the principal-office field when the arrangement is legitimate and activated before filing. That can create useful separation between home and business for a Broward entrepreneur. It is not a promise that a home address will never appear anywhere in public records.

Addresses may appear in prior records, licenses, court documents, or entries for managers and authorized representatives. Review the complete filing. If privacy or safety is a major concern, ask a Florida attorney to evaluate the full record.

Checklist Before You File or Update Your LLC

  1. Confirm the legal name. Use the LLC name exactly as it will appear in the Articles of Organization.
  2. Activate the business address first. Do not publish or file a provider's address before onboarding is complete and use is authorized.
  3. Read the agreement. Confirm that it covers the specific public business use you intend. Keep the signed address agreement banks commonly ask for with your records.
  4. Choose the mailing address deliberately. Decide where time-sensitive correspondence can be received and held safely and collected promptly.
  5. Appoint the registered agent separately. Verify eligibility, Florida street address, availability, and consent before entering the agent's information.
  6. Check industry rules. Real estate, health, financial, legal, construction, and other regulated businesses may have requirements beyond the basic LLC filing.
  7. Review every public field. Look for residential addresses in manager, authorized-representative, and other entries if privacy is a goal.
  8. Keep the record current. When the business or agent moves, use the appropriate Sunbiz process for the field that changed.

What Comes After Your Sunbiz Filing?

Forming the LLC and applying for a federal tax ID are separate steps. Before completing Form SS-4, read how the IRS mailing, street-address, and principal-business fields work for an EIN. Do not assume the entry used on Sunbiz belongs in every IRS field.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every Florida LLC need a principal address and a registered agent?

A Florida LLC filing identifies a principal office and a registered agent with a registered office. The same street location may appear in both fields when each use independently qualifies, but the roles and requirements remain distinct.

Can I use a P.O. Box for my Florida LLC?

Sunbiz says a P.O. Box is acceptable for the mailing address. Its instructions describe the principal office as a street address and prohibit a P.O. Box for the registered agent’s Florida address.

Can a business address be my Florida LLC principal address?

It may be when the LLC is authorized to use the address and the entry accurately reflects the company’s circumstances. Confirm the written agreement and any licensing or industry-specific rules before filing.

Does a business-address plan include registered-agent service?

Not automatically. A business address establishes an authorized professional address under an agreement. Registered-agent service requires a qualified agent, a Florida registered office, consent to the appointment, and performance of a separate legal function.
