What Is a Florida Registered Agent?

The legal requirement every Florida LLC and corporation must meet.

Every Florida LLC and corporation is required by state law to designate a registered agent. This is not optional. Florida Statute 605.0113 (for LLCs) and Florida Statute 607.0501 (for corporations) mandate that every business entity maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in Florida where legal documents can be delivered during normal business hours.

A registered agent serves as the official point of contact between your business and the state. When someone files a lawsuit against your company, the complaint and summons are delivered to your registered agent. When the Florida Division of Corporations sends annual report notices, they go to your registered agent. When any government agency needs to communicate with your business entity, the registered agent receives that communication.

If your LLC or corporation fails to maintain a registered agent, the consequences are serious. The Florida Division of Corporations can administratively dissolve your LLC or revoke your corporation's authority to do business in the state. Reinstatement requires additional filings and fees, and during the period of dissolution, your liability protection may be compromised.

Why You Should Not Be Your Own Registered Agent

Florida law allows you to serve as your own registered agent if you are a Florida resident. Many business owners do this when they first form their LLC, listing their home address on the Articles of Organization. This creates three problems:

Your home address becomes public record. The Florida Division of Corporations maintains a searchable online database. Anyone can look up your LLC and see your registered agent address. If that address is your home, your personal residence is exposed to the public, including process servers, solicitors, and anyone else who searches for your business.

You must be available during all business hours. A registered agent must be present at the registered address during normal business hours to accept service of process. If you travel, work remotely, or simply step out for lunch when a process server arrives, service may be attempted through alternative means, potentially without your knowledge until it is too late to respond.

You cannot use a P.O. Box. The registered agent address must be a physical street address where documents can be personally delivered. P.O. Boxes, UPS Store addresses, and virtual mailboxes that are clearly identified as such are not accepted by the Division of Corporations for registered agent purposes.

Online registered agent services have become popular in recent years. Companies like Northwest Registered Agent, LegalZoom, and Incfile offer registered agent services for $49 to $299 per year. These services work, but they have limitations that physical locations do not.

Service of process requires physical acceptance. When a process server delivers a lawsuit, someone must physically accept the document. Online-only providers use remote offices or contracted acceptance agents who may not be at the address full-time. A physical office like Wilton Plaza has staff on site during all business hours to accept service immediately.

State filings show the address. The address listed on your Articles of Organization and annual report is visible to clients, vendors, banks, and licensing boards. A commercial office building address at 1881 NE 26th St, Wilton Manors, FL 33305 carries more credibility than a virtual address associated with a nationwide registered agent factory.

You can walk in. Need to pick up a document, get something notarized, or discuss your account? At a physical location, you drive over and handle it. With an online-only provider, everything goes through email or phone trees.

Florida Registered Agent FAQ

What is a Florida Registered Agent and why do I need one?
A Florida Registered Agent is a person or company designated to receive legal documents, service of process, and official state correspondence on behalf of your business. Florida law (F.S. 605.0113 for LLCs, F.S. 607.0501 for corporations) requires every Florida LLC and corporation to maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in Florida. You cannot use a P.O. Box. If you fail to maintain a registered agent, the state can administratively dissolve your LLC.
