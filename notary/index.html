What We Cannot Notarize

Florida law (Chapter 117) prohibits notarization in certain situations. Tricia will decline to notarize:

Documents where the notary has a financial interest. If Tricia is a party to the transaction, she cannot notarize it.

Documents signed by someone who is not physically present. Every signer must appear in person before the notary.

Documents presented without proper identification. A valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID is required for every signer.

Blank or incomplete documents. All fields must be completed before notarization. Do not bring documents with blank spaces to be filled in later.

Suspected fraud. Per F.S. 117.107, if a notary has reasonable grounds to believe a transaction is fraudulent, they must refuse to notarize.

Real estate closing documents. We do not notarize deeds, mortgages, or closing packages.

Immigration documents. We do not notarize I-9 forms, travel authorizations, or other immigration-related documents.

Vital records. Birth certificates, death certificates, and other vital records cannot be notarized.

Yes. Documents in Spanish or other languages can be notarized, but the notarial acknowledgment and certificate must be in English per Florida law.
